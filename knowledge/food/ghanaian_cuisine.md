# Ghanaian Cuisine

## Overview
Ghanaian food is flavorful, hearty, and diverse. Staples include starchy roots, grains, and plantains served with soups, stews, and grilled proteins.

## Classic Dishes

### Fufu & Light Soup
- **Description**: Pounded cassava and plantain (or yam) served with a light tomato-based soup with meat or fish
- **How to eat**: Using the right hand, form a small ball of fufu, dip in soup, and swallow without chewing
- **Where to try**: Any local chop bar; Fafaa Restaurant (Accra)

### Jollof Rice
- **Description**: Rice cooked in tomato, onion, and pepper sauce with spices. Served with chicken, beef, or fish
- **Ghanaian vs Nigerian**: Ghanaians use more tomato and less pepper; jollof wars are a friendly rivalry
- **Where to try**: Azmera Restaurant (Osu, Accra), Vic's Cafe (Kumasi)

### Banku & Tilapia
- **Description**: Fermented corn and cassava dough (banku) served with grilled tilapia, pepper sauce, and shito (black pepper sauce)
- **Where to try**: Labadi Beach (weekends), Mama Africa Restaurant (Cape Coast)

### Red Red
- **Description**: Black-eyed peas cooked in palm oil, served with fried plantains and gari
- **Price**: 20–40 GHS
- **Best as**: A budget-friendly, filling meal

### Kenkey & Fish
- **Description**: Fermented corn dumpling (kenkey) served with fried fish and pepper sauce
- **Varieties**: Ga kenkey (white, sour) vs. Fante kenkey (brown, less sour)
- **Where to try**: Street food stalls in Jamestown (Accra)

### Waakye
- **Description**: Rice and beans cooked together, served with spaghetti, gari, egg, meat, and shito
- **Best as**: Breakfast or lunch
- **Price**: 15–30 GHS
- **Where to try**: Street vendors everywhere, Blue Gate Waakye (Accra)

### Kelewele
- **Description**: Spiced fried plantain cubes with ginger, pepper, and onions
- **Best as**: Street snack, often sold at night
- **Price**: 5–15 GHS

## Local Beverages
- **Sobolo** — Hibiscus tea, sweet/spiced, non-alcoholic
- **Palm Wine** — Fermented palm tree sap, mildly alcoholic, very refreshing
- **Akpeteshie** — Local gin (strong!), distilled from palm wine or sugar cane
- **Asaana** — Sweet corn drink from the Central Region
- **Malt drinks** — Malta Guinness, Club Malt (non-alcoholic)
