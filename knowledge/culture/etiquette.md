# Ghanaian Etiquette — A Guide for Visitors

## Greetings
- **Always greet** before starting a conversation — skipping greetings is considered rude
- Handshake is standard: grip firmly but gently, often prolonged while exchanging pleasantries
- **Right hand only** for handshakes, giving/receiving items, and eating
- The left hand is considered unclean — avoid pointing, gesturing, or passing items with it
- Common greetings: "Akwaaba" (Twi — Welcome), "Maakye" (Good morning), "Maaha" (Good afternoon), "Maadwo" (Good evening)

## Addressing People
- Use titles: Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr., Chief, or "Nana" (chief/elder)
- When in doubt, use "Sir" or "Madam" — especially with older people
- Age is respected — always acknowledge elders first in a group

## Dress Code
- Modest dress is appreciated, especially outside Accra
- Avoid shorts and revealing clothing in rural areas, markets, and religious sites
- For formal occasions, traditional attire (batakari/smock for men, kente cloth for women) is welcome
- Beachwear is fine on the beach, not in town

## Hospitality
- Ghanaians are famously hospitable — expect to be offered food or drink
- It's polite to accept at least a small offering; refusing completely can offend
- If invited to someone's home, bring a small gift (bread, fruit, soft drink)
- Shoes are typically removed before entering a home

## Time & Punctuality
- "Ghana Time" is a real concept — events often start 30–60 min late
- However, show up on time for official appointments and business meetings
- For social events, arriving 30 min late is normal

## Dining Etiquette
- Wash hands before eating (a bowl of water is often provided)
- Eat with the right hand only
- Wait for the eldest or host to begin eating first
- Compliment the food — it's appreciated
- Avoid finishing every last grain of rice on your plate (it signals you're still hungry)

## Photos
- Always ask permission before photographing people
- Some sacred sites and ceremonies prohibit photography
- Government buildings and military installations: no photos

## Bargaining
- Expected in markets but not in shops with fixed prices
- Start at ~50% of the quoted price, settle around 60–70%
- Bargain with a smile and good humor — it's a social exchange
