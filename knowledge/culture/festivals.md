# Festivals of Ghana

## Overview
Ghana has over 70 distinct ethnic groups, each with their own festivals. Most are tied to the harvest season, ancestral commemoration, or chieftaincy ceremonies.

## Major Festivals

### Homowo (Ga People — Greater Accra)
- **When**: May (monthly schedule varies by town)
- **What**: "Hooting at hunger" harvest festival with processions, drumming, dancing, and the traditional dish *kpokploi*
- **Where**: Accra, Teshie, Nungua, and other Ga towns
- **Traveler tip**: The Friday street procession is the most colorful — arrive early for a good spot

### Aboakyer (Effutu People — Winneba)
- **When**: First Saturday in May
- **What**: "Animal hunting" festival where two groups compete to catch a live antelope
- **Where**: Winneba (Central Region, 1.5 hrs from Accra)
- **Traveler tip**: This is an intense, crowded event — keep a safe distance from the hunt

### Odwira (Akuapem People — Akropong)
- **When**: September–October (dates announced yearly)
- **What**: Purification and harvest festival with durbar of chiefs, drumming, and dancing
- **Where**: Akropong (Eastern Region)
- **Traveler tip**: The grand durbar (Saturday) features spectacular kente and gold regalia

### Panafest
- **When**: Every two years (next: 2025), usually July–August
- **What**: Pan-African Historical Theatre Festival — celebrates African culture and diaspora heritage
- **Where**: Cape Coast and Elmina
- **Traveler tip**: Book accommodation months in advance — it draws international crowds

### Chale Wote Street Art Festival
- **When**: August
- **What**: Alternative street art, music, and performance festival in Accra
- **Where**: James Town, Accra
- **Traveler tip**: Wear comfortable shoes, bring water, and prepare for crowds

### Akwasidae (Ashanti — Kumasi)
- **When**: Every 42 days (6 times per year)
- **What**: Sacred Asante celebration at the Manhyia Palace honoring the Golden Stool
- **Where**: Manhyia Palace, Kumasi
- **Traveler tip**: A profound cultural experience. Dress modestly. No photography inside the palace

### Hogbetsotso (Anlo Ewe People — Volta Region)
- **When**: First Saturday in November
- **What**: "Festival of Exodus" — commemorates the Ewe migration from Notsie (Togo)
- **Where**: Anloga (Volta Region)
- **Traveler tip**: Colorful processions and traditional music — best experienced with a local guide
